Amazon Growth Strategy

Prepared by FTR  ·  Amazon & Walmart Growth

Overview

Dead Down Wind is entering the 2026 hunting season with strong momentum. Jan–Jul Amazon sales are approximately 74% ahead of the same period in 2025, while branded demand and overall search visibility have also increased.

The opportunity now is to make sure DDW is fully positioned to capitalize on the upcoming peak, while continuing to expand beyond customers already searching specifically for the brand.

This is especially important given how seasonal the business is. Approximately 86% of 2025 Amazon revenue was generated between August and December, with demand accelerating significantly from August and reaching its highest levels in October and November.

The strategy will focus on three key Growth Areas:

Growth Area 1: Maximize Peak-Season Demand Capture

Ensure DDW is fully ready to capture the upcoming seasonal demand by addressing:

  • Featured Offer issues (buybox ownership)
  • Protecting inventory, scaling PPC alongside growing search volume
  • Activating Brand Tailored Promotions
  • Planning Amazon event participation
Growth Area 2: Expand Non-Branded Customer Acquisition

Protect DDW's strong branded position while reaching more new customers through high-intent, non-branded search, PPC and Creator Connections.

Growth Area 3: Strengthen the DDW Amazon Shopping Experience
  • Prioritize the highest-impact ASINs for optimization
  • Expand A+ and video utilization
  • Improve the Store experience
  • Communicate more clearly DDW's complete scent-control system across Amazon

The three Growth Areas will be supported by a Seasonal Amazon Growth Framework, providing a clear direction for the account throughout each stage of DDW's seasonal cycle.

Growth Area 1: Maximize Peak-Season Demand Capture
Opportunity

DDW's biggest immediate opportunity is to make sure the brand is fully prepared to capture the demand already entering Amazon.

Search visibility increases dramatically through Q3 and Q4, but several current gaps could limit how much of that demand DDW converts into sales.

1. Fix Buybox Ownership

Featured Offer ownership is inconsistent across several key products, including:

  • B0060CSIB0 - Wind Detector
  • B07D8J6GQB - Unscented Laundry Detergent

Both are strong-performing products with meaningful search opportunity, making Featured Offer loss particularly concerning as DDW enters peak season.

Key Recommendations

Identify and address Featured Offer issues across priority ASINs and establish ongoing monitoring throughout peak season.

2. Prioritize the Peak-Season Hero Products

Priority products include:

  • Hunt Ready 10-Piece Kit
  • Unscented Laundry Detergent
  • Dryer Sheets
  • Wash Cloths
  • Natural Woods Laundry products
  • BLACK Premium Body Wash
  • BLACK Premium Field Spray
  • Field Spray + Refill products

Classify the products into:

  • Hero - highest sales/search opportunity, maximum investment
  • Growth - rapidly gaining traction, prioritize expansion
  • Maintain - profitable supporting products
  • Exit - products being replaced or discontinued
3. Scale PPC With Seasonal Demand

PPC should follow the seasonal demand trend, with investment increasing before and throughout the peak.

Focus on:

  • Protecting branded demand
  • Expanding high-intent non-branded coverage
  • Prioritizing Hero and Growth ASINs
  • Scaling alongside search-volume growth
  • Avoiding aggressive traffic investment on products with unresolved inventory or Featured Offer issues
  • Reducing spend as seasonal demand declines
4. Activate Brand Tailored Promotions During Peak

Use Brand Tailored Promotions during peak season to reach relevant audiences and support conversion.

Key Recommendations

Activate BTP across relevant audiences, including High Intent Customers, Cart Abandoners and High Potential New Customers.

Monitor performance throughout the season and optimize based on results.

5. Align Inventory With the Seasonal Demand

Inventory planning needs to reflect clear seasonal shifts.

The goal is to maintain enough inventory to capture peak demand without carrying unnecessary hunting inventory into the off-season.

Growth Area 2: Expand Non-Branded Customer Acquisition
Opportunity

DDW already has strong brand recognition on Amazon, with a significant share of sales coming from customers actively searching for the brand.

The next opportunity is to expand reach among hunters searching for relevant products and solutions who have not yet selected a brand.

1. Protect Branded Search

Maintain strong coverage across core branded searches such as:

  • dead down wind
  • dead down wind laundry detergent
  • dead down wind spray
  • dead down wind field spray
  • dead down wind dryer sheets
2. Grow High-Intent Non-Branded Search Share

The goal should not be to compete aggressively on broad searches such as laundry detergent / body wash / dryer sheets, where DDW would compete against mainstream household brands and much broader shopper intent.

Instead, prioritize relevant hunting searches such as:

hunting laundry detergent scent killer for hunting hunting scent eliminator hunting dryer sheets hunting deodorant wind checker hunting wind checker wind detector for hunting

These searches reach shoppers who already understand the problem but have not yet purchased from DDW.

3. Recover Lost Share on Proven Search Terms

DDW already has meaningful relevance across several high-intent non-branded searches but has lost purchase share YoY, including terms such as:

  • hunting laundry detergent
  • scent killer for hunting
  • hunting scent eliminator

Prioritize proven search terms where DDW has an opportunity to recover and grow share.

4. Scale Emerging Non-Branded Wins

The Wind Detector is showing strong growth across relevant non-branded searches, including

  • wind checker
  • hunting wind checker
  • wind detector for hunting

Identify what is driving this growth and apply relevant learnings across other priority products.

5. Advertising Strategy

What we found:

  1. Auto campaigns are doing most of the work. About 70% of ad spend and 75% of sales are coming from auto campaigns. Those are useful for discovery, but they can't be aimed. The manual, targeted campaigns that let us control exactly which searches you show up for are currently a small part of the mix.
  2. A large share of sales is coming from DDW brand name. Shoppers already searching "Dead Down Wind" account for roughly 42% of ad sales at a very efficient cost. To grow we need to explore more of the non-branded side.
  3. Tinks is quiet heading into its peak season. Tinks has had very little advertising support over this window. Given that Q3/Q4 is the heart of the Tinks season, this is a big near term opportunity in the account.
  4. The newer categories aren't advertising yet. Defend, Smith & Wesson, and the spray lines don't currently have active campaigns. Defend especially stands out since it's Amazon only with no retail price conflict.
  5. Bear spray is getting clicks but few sales. That points to a listing/content opportunity rather than an advertising one, so we'll want the product pages in good shape before we put a real budget behind it.
Ads Strategy

Move the account off autopilot. Right now automatic campaigns drive most of the results. We'll build a deliberate, brand by brand structure underneath so Q3/Q4 is driven by intention.

Three priorities, in order:

  1. Protect what's working: Dead Down Wind is performing; lock in its branded and top terms.
  2. Win the season: get Tinks fully built before its Q3/Q4 peak.
  3. Grow the new lines: scale Defend, Smith & Wesson and the sprays once listings are ready, leading with Defend where we can push hardest.

6. Launch Creator Connections

Creator Connections is currently not being utilized despite DDW being a strong fit for creator-led acquisition.

DDW is a strong fit for creator-led content, with products that are easy to demonstrate and existing relationships with hunting creators that can be leveraged on Amazon.

Rather than simply promoting individual products, creator content should reinforce the complete DDW routine:

Laundry → Hygiene → Gear → Field

Potential direction:

  • "How I prepare for hunting season"
  • "My scent-control routine before a hunt"
  • Product demonstrations / how-to content

Launch Creator Connections during the seasonal ramp and prioritize Hero/Growth products with stable inventory and Featured Offer ownership.

Defend Product Line

Defend is a newer product line within the Dead Down Wind Amazon structure. The immediate priority is to resolve catalog issues across the full line while strengthening and growing the currently active Bear Spray and Pepper Spray Gel product families.

1. Clean Up & Establish the Defend Catalog
  • Audit the full Defend catalog and confirm the status of every ASIN.
  • Resolve inactive, suppressed, blocked, or compliance-related issues, particularly across the insect protection range.
  • Ensure applicable product claims and content align with EPA labeling requirements.
  • For Bear Spray and Pepper Spray Gel, restructure eligible ASINs into logical parent/variation families.
2. Build an Intentional Customer Journey
  • Re-optimize Bear Spray and Pepper Spray Gel listings across SEO, Main Images, image stacks, A+, and video.
  • Clearly communicate differences between sizes, formats, accessories, and use cases.
  • Use variations, A+, Brand Story, and the Defend section of the DDW Store to connect products and guide shoppers through the range.
  • Strengthen product comparison, cross-linking, and cross-selling between relevant Defend products.
3. Establish Trust & Launch Support
  • Build the initial review foundation across the active Defend ASINs, including Amazon Vine where eligible.
  • Activate Creator Connections for priority products, particularly given the higher price points and importance of product demonstration.
  • Use creator/video content to strengthen product education and purchase confidence.
  • Scale acquisition as reviews, conversion, and sales history develop.
4. Advertising Strategy

Advertising for Defend is deliberately sequenced to follow catalog cleanup, listing optimization, and the initial review foundation. Because the active products carry higher price points and limited sales history, driving paid traffic before listings and reviews are ready would spend inefficiently against pages that are not yet positioned to convert. Investment scales as conversion and trust develop across the active Bear Spray and Pepper Spray Gel families.

Defend's position within the category is an advantage: most products are Amazon exclusive, with no brick-and-mortar pricing conflict, which allows for more aggressive visibility and pricing strategy than the retailer-sensitive brands; subject to approval on any pricing moves and full EPA compliance on all claims.

Recommendations
  • Sequence advertising behind launch readiness. Hold meaningful investment until Bear Spray and Pepper Spray Gel listings are optimized and an initial review base is in place, so traffic converts rather than burns against unproven pages.
  • Start controlled, then scale. Begin with tightly targeted campaigns on the highest-intent searches for each active family, expanding investment as conversion and review depth improve.
  • Align ads to the priority searches. Point advertising at the same high intent non-branded searches identified for SEO (bear spray, pepper spray, and the specific formats and sizes we are prioritizing), keeping paid and organic working together.
  • Use Defend's exclusivity as a lever. Where margin and approval allow, use Defend's Amazon exclusive position to be more aggressive on visibility and promotional pricing than is possible for the retail sensitive brands.
  • Capture competitor demand. Establish deliberate targeting against category competitors (Sawyer, Ben's, Natrapel, HUNT) as listings become conversion ready.
  • Confirm true selling seasons before scaling. Use competitor and category data to validate the real demand windows for bear spray, pepper spray, and insect protection, and align investment to those windows rather than assumed seasonality.

Advertising will be managed to Defend's efficiency guardrails, with a bias toward building efficiently first and scaling as the brand's conversion and review foundation matures.

5. Creative Direction
The Core Shift

Defend's website and messaging are built around one word: protection. Right now that likely reads as fairly functional/product-forward, which makes sense for a new brand establishing credibility. On Amazon, we want to keep the confidence and practicality but make it feel lived-in — real people using this stuff in real outdoor moments, not a spray bottle on a white background making a claim.

The brand isn't "hunting gear." It's "outdoor confidence." Hunting, hiking, camping, family trips, yard work — the product line spans all of it. Creative needs to reflect that breadth without diluting into generic lifestyle stock.

Visual Concept

"Prepared, not paranoid."

The customer isn't buying because they're scared of the outdoors — they're buying so fear never enters the equation. Every image should communicate ease and readiness, not danger or emergency. Avoid anything that feels like a warning label come to life (no dramatic bear-attack-adjacent imagery, no fear-based staging). The threat is implied, the confidence is shown.

Photography Direction

Tone: Warm, natural light. Golden hour or soft daylight — not studio-flat, not overly moody/dark like a tactical gear brand. This isn't Dead Down Wind's cinematic hunt-prep aesthetic; it's brighter and more accessible, because the audience is broader (families, hikers, everyday outdoor users, not just hunters).

Composition: Product integrated into the moment, not posed next to it. Someone clipping bear spray to a pack before a hike. A hand applying repellent mid-stride on a trail, not standing still staring at the bottle. Kids running ahead on a campsite path while a parent reaches for the spray without breaking stride — protection as a background habit, not a foreground task.

Settings to feature (based on their stated customer range):

  • Trailhead / hiking path
  • Campsite (tent, cooler, dog nearby)
  • Backyard / yard work (a genuinely underused Amazon angle for this category — most competitors go straight to deep woods)
  • Truck bed / tailgate prep shots (gear-check moment before heading out)
  • Water/boat dock for fishing angle
  • Family outdoor scenes — not staged "family portrait," more candid mid-activity

What to avoid:

  • Studio white-background-only lifestyle shots (fine for main image, not for secondary/A+ storytelling)
  • Overly tactical/military styling — the brand voice is "confident and practical," not aggressive
  • Fear-forward visuals (bug close-ups dripping menace, snarling wildlife) — use these sparingly if at all, and only as small supporting graphics, not hero imagery
  • All-male casting — brand explicitly wants gender-neutral representation
Color & Mood

Since the brand guide's exact palette needs confirming from their file, direction should lean: natural, earthy outdoor tones (greens, browns, warm neutrals) with clean orange and yellow accent colors for CTAs/callouts that reads modern rather than "classic hunting brand." Given this sits under Arcus Hunting alongside Dead Down Wind, Defend should feel like a sibling brand.

Graphic Treatment
  • Icon system for callouts (EPA-registered, DEET-free/picaridin, protection duration, etc.) — clean, simple line icons, not heavily illustrated
  • Minimal overlay text on lifestyle shots — let the moment read first, add a short benefit callout, don't caption the whole scene
  • Comparison/education graphics (repellent types, application guidance) should feel like quick visual reference, not a wall of text — this audience wants clarity fast
Product Styling
  • Show scale — hand-held, clipped to a pack, in a pocket — so shoppers immediately understand size/portability, a stated pain point ("don't want products that are difficult to carry")
  • Label/cap visible enough to read key claims at a glance
  • For sprays specifically: show the actual motion of use once (application demo image) — removes ambiguity, hits the FAQ concern about "how do I use this"
What This Achieves for the Shopper Journey
  • Main image: clean product truth, maybe one applied-benefit callout
  • Secondary images: this is where "prepared, not paranoid" lives — real settings, real use, benefit callouts tied to the pain points (long-lasting protection, easy to carry, family-safe, EPA-verified)
  • A+/Brand Story: broaden out to the full outdoor lifestyle story — this is the moment to show the range of settings (not just hunting) and build the "Arcus Hunting heritage = trustworthy formulation" trust layer
  • Storefront: organize by threat solved (insects/ticks, wildlife, personal protection) rather than by product type — matches how the customer actually thinks about the problem

Creative Direction
Brand Positioning

Dead Down Wind is the scent-control system built for hunters who take preparation seriously - not a single product, but a full routine from home to field. The core differentiator is elimination vs. masking: competitors cover scent, DDW destroys it (ScentPrevent enzyme tech + NidorX compound).

We need to position ourselves as the expert and leader in this niche, promoting brand trust through consistent imagery that feels professional and premium-but-attainable.

Competitors on Amazon significantly lack branding consistency throughout their listings, with many having few images and little to no A+ content.

There's a lot of opportunity for us to immediately position ourselves above these competitors by building out a comprehensive listing that not only educates consumers, but also allows them to envision incorporating the products into their lives.

Target Shopper

Primarily male, 25–64, household income $75K–$99K. Serious outdoorsman - whitetail hunting is core, but also fishes, camps, hikes, shoots. Researches gear before buying, follows hunting content/communities, wants to trust that what he buys will actually work.

He's not a scent-control expert, but he's not casual either - he wants confidence that he's done everything he can before he's in the field.

As the target audience is primarily male, we should incorporate a more premium masculine feel to the listings with darker tones and more male lifestyle models, while still carrying the distinctive brighter orange and yellow of the brand throughout.

Shopper Problem

He doesn't know if his current scent-control routine (regular detergent, cover scent, wind-checking) is actually working - and that uncertainty costs him confidence, and potentially game. He needs proof that DDW eliminates odor rather than just masking it, and he needs to see it fits into a routine he can actually stick to.

Main Conversion Opportunities
  • Amazon's hunting/outdoor category is visually weak and inconsistent - polished, cohesive creative alone will separate DDW from shelf clutter.
  • Main image and image 2–3 need to lead with the elimination-vs-masking USP, not general lifestyle. This is the single biggest thing standing between a scroll and a click.
  • Full-funnel storytelling (prep at home → gear → field) is underused in this category - most competitors show either product-only or field-only. Owning the "complete system" angle is a real gap.
  • Trust signals (real science positioning, tech names, straightforward no-nonsense tone) need visual weight - this shopper responds to credibility, not hype.
Visual Direction

Rugged, authentic, premium - serious hunting meets the everyday ritual of scent control. Warm, cinematic photography with deep shadows, natural woodland tones (rich browns, greens), and DDW's signature orange/yellow as accent, not wallpaper. Real moments of preparation - showering, applying product, prepping clothing/gear - shown alongside the hunter in the field, so the full journey reads at a glance. Masculine, confident, practical, no-nonsense - a hunter who trusts the science and takes prep seriously.

Photography Style

Cinematic, naturalistic lighting with deep shadow contrast - not bright, amateur-photography lighting. Shallow depth of field on product close-ups to keep a tactile, premium feel. Product shots integrated into real environments (locker room, mudroom, truck bed, treestand) rather than isolated studio product-on-white, except where Amazon requires a clean main image.

Graphic Style

Callouts should be overlayed to fully educate the consumer, utilizing existing brand guide fonts. Icons should match the existing ScentPrevent/NidorX icon system already in the brand guide.

Consumer Buying Journey

We want to create a clear, directional journey for consumers to educate and build trust - through listing images, A+ content, brand story, and store.

Incorporating cross-selling through display of product range in all elements will be beneficial.

Premium but Attainable

The cinematic, deep-shadow photography direction reads premium without reading expensive - it's aspirational in tone (serious hunter, taken-seriously prep) rather than aspirational in price point. Keep product-in-use shots grounded in real, slightly worn environments (garage, truck, gear bag) rather than overly polished sets - that's what keeps it feeling accessible to an everyday hunter rather than a luxury outdoor brand.
